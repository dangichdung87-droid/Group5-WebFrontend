Phở

The aromatic soul and national pride of Vietnam.

A golden, crystal-clear broth simmered for hours from beef marrow bones, charred ginger, star anise, and cinnamon. Poured piping hot over silky flat rice noodles (bánh phở) and tender beef cuts, every bowl offers a delicate yet unforgettable harmony of flavors.

The dish at a glance

A Masterpiece of Balance and Patience

Unlike heavily spiced or oily noodle soups, authentic Vietnamese Phở is celebrated worldwide for its pure, nuanced broth and restorative lightness. It requires hours of gentle simmering, precise aromatic charring, and fresh herbal garnishes to achieve its signature harmony.

Slow-Simmered Broth

Beef marrow bones simmered gently for 6 to 8 hours with charred ginger, shallots, star anise, and cassia cinnamon for absolute clarity and depth.

Silky Rice Noodles

Fresh, artisanal flat rice noodles (bánh phở) blanched just before serving, delivering a soft yet delicate chew that absorbs the savory broth.

Fresh Aromatics

Crisp scallions, sweet white onion slices, sawtooth herb (ngò gai), Thai basil, lime wedges, and bird's eye chilies to brighten every spoonful.

Nam Định & Hanoi · Early 20th Century

Born on the
streets of the North.

Phở took shape in northern Vietnam in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, primarily around Nam Định province and Hanoi's bustling Old Quarter. It developed as a creative crossroads where Vietnamese rice noodle craftsmanship met French influences, notably the introduction of beef cuts and slow-simmered bone bouillon (similar to pot-au-feu).

In the early days, phở was carried through winding Hanoi alleys by itinerant vendors carrying bamboo shoulder poles known as gánh phở. Balanced on one end was a wood-fired brazier holding the steaming broth cauldron, and on the other were noodles, bowls, meats, and spices. Today, Phở is internationally celebrated by culinary critics and honored as part of Vietnam's National Intangible Cultural Heritage.

Regional Traditions

Phở Bắc vs. Phở Nam: Two Distinct Traditions

As phở traveled southward in the mid-20th century, regional tastes gave rise to two beloved interpretations, each with its own devoted following.

Phở Bắc (Northern Style)

Hanoi Purist

Celebrated for its clarity and understated elegance. The broth is clear, delicately seasoned with fish sauce and whole spices, focusing on the pure natural sweetness of beef bones.

  • Broth: Clear, subtle, lightly spiced, minimal sweetness.
  • Accompaniments: Pickled garlic vinegar, fresh chili, and crispy fried crullers (quẩy).
  • Greens: Focused on scallions and fresh cilantro; rarely served with bean sprouts or sweet bean sauce.

Phở Nam (Southern Style)

Saigon Vibrant

A bolder, sweeter, and more exuberant rendition. The broth is often richer, slightly caramelized with rock sugar, and served with a lavish garden of fresh herbs and condiment sauces.

  • Broth: Sweeter, darker, infused with roasted spices and sometimes charred shallots.
  • Accompaniments: Fresh lime wedges, sliced jalapeños or bird's eye chilies, hoisin sauce (tương đen), and sriracha.
  • Greens: Piles of crunchy bean sprouts, Thai basil (húng quế), and sawtooth herb (ngò gai).

From Hanoi to your kitchen

Cook Traditional Beef Phở (Phở Bò) at Home

A master recipe for authentic Northern-style Phở Bò. The secret lies in blanching the bones thoroughly and simmering at a gentle roll without boiling rapidly, ensuring crystal broth clarity.

Serves
4–6 people
Preparation
About 30 minutes
Simmering
6 to 8 hours
Assembly
About 15 minutes

Ingredients

Broth & Bones

  • 1.5 kg beef marrow bones and knuckle bones
  • 500 g beef brisket or flank (for cooked beef slices)
  • 4.5 litres fresh water
  • 3 tbsp premium fish sauce (nước mắm)
  • 25 g yellow rock sugar (đường phèn)
  • 1 tbsp sea salt

Charred Aromatics & Spices

  • 1 large ginger hand (approx. 80 g), unpeeled
  • 4 large shallots, unpeeled
  • 1 medium yellow onion, halved
  • 5 whole star anise pods
  • 1 cinnamon stick (cassia bark)
  • 1 black cardamom pod (thảo quả), lightly cracked
  • 3 cloves & 1 tsp coriander seeds

Noodles & Beef Cuts

  • 500 g fresh flat rice noodles (bánh phở)
  • 250 g beef tenderloin or eye of round, paper-thinly sliced (for rare beef - phở tái)

Garnishes & Table Serving

  • 1 small white onion, shaved paper-thin and soaked in ice water
  • 1 bunch scallions (green onions), finely chopped
  • 1 bunch fresh cilantro & culantro (ngò gai)
  • Fresh lime wedges & bird's eye chili slices
  • Pickled garlic vinegar or chili sauce to taste

Step by step

  1. Parboil and wash the beef bones

    Place the beef bones into a large stockpot and submerge under cold water. Bring to a vigorous rolling boil for 8–10 minutes to release coagulated proteins and impurities. Drain completely, then rinse each bone thoroughly under warm running water. Scrub the pot clean before continuing.

  2. Char the aromatics & toast the spices

    Char the ginger, shallots, and onion halves directly over an open flame or in a dry cast-iron skillet until blackened spots appear. Peel off the loose charred skins and rinse lightly. In a dry pan over medium-low heat, gently toast the star anise, cinnamon stick, black cardamom, cloves, and coriander seeds for 2–3 minutes until deeply fragrant. Place spices into a spice bag or cheesecloth pouch.

  3. Slow-simmer the golden broth

    Return the cleaned bones and the beef brisket to the pot with 4.5 litres of fresh water. Bring to a gentle boil, then immediately turn the heat to the lowest setting for a bare simmer. Add the charred ginger, shallots, rock sugar, and salt. Cook uncovered for 6 to 8 hours, frequently skimming any foam from the surface. Add the spice bag during the final 90 minutes of simmering so the spices remain fragrant without overpowering the stock.

  4. Rest the cooked meat and strain broth

    Remove the cooked beef brisket once tender (after about 2–2.5 hours), submerge in cold water for 5 minutes to prevent darkening, and slice thinly. When broth simmering is complete, discard the bones and aromatics. Strain the broth through a fine-mesh sieve lined with cheesecloth. Season to taste with fish sauce and salt.

  5. Warm bowls & blanch the rice noodles

    Bring a separate pot of water to a boil. Blanch the fresh bánh phở noodles for 15–20 seconds using a noodle strainer basket. Shake off excess water and divide immediately into warmed deep serving bowls.

  6. Assemble and ladle piping hot

    Arrange the cooked brisket slices and paper-thin raw beef slices over the noodles. Scatter chopped scallions, shaved white onion, and cilantro across the top. Bring the broth to a vigorous rolling boil, then ladle directly over the raw beef to flash-cook it to tender pink perfection. Serve immediately with fresh limes and chilies.

The tasting ritual

How to Savour Every Drop of Phở

01

Sip the pure broth first

Before adding sauces or squeezing lime, take a spoonful of broth to appreciate the pure, multifaceted depth achieved from bone marrow and roasted spices.

02

Gently layer aromatics

Squeeze a fresh lime wedge and tear fresh herbs (ngò gai, Thai basil) with your fingers to release essential oils into the steaming bowl.

03

Dip rather than drown

Place hoisin or chili sauce in a small dipping dish on the side. Dip pieces of tender beef into the sauce rather than altering the color and purity of the artisan broth.
