Bún Chả

A taste of Hanoi, fresh off the grill.

Smoky pork, delicate rice noodles, crisp herbs, and a sweet-tangy dipping sauce. Bún Chả brings contrasting flavors and textures together in one wonderfully balanced meal.

Try the recipe The Obama connection

The dish at a glance

A Hanoi lunchtime favorite

Bún Chả is closely associated with Hanoi's street-food culture. Pork patties (chả viên) and slices of pork (chả miếng) are grilled and served with rice vermicelli (bún). A bowl of diluted fish-sauce dressing brings sweetness, acidity, and savory depth, while pickled vegetables and fresh herbs keep each bite bright.

Smoky & savory

Charcoal grilling gives the marinated pork its fragrant aroma and caramelized edges.

Fresh & fragrant

Soft rice noodles meet lettuce, mint, coriander, and other fresh herbs.

Sweet & tangy

Fish sauce, sugar, and a little acidity form the dipping sauce that ties the meal together.

A familiar dish.
A famous guest.

During his 2016 visit to Vietnam, then-U.S. President Barack Obama shared a meal with chef and television host Anthony Bourdain at Bún Chả Hương Liên in Hanoi. Their dinner was filmed for Bourdain's show Parts Unknown, putting a spotlight on this everyday Hanoi favorite.

From Hanoi to your kitchen

Make Bún Chả at home

A home-style version with both pork patties and pork slices. A grill pan works well if you do not have a charcoal grill. Quantities and cooking times are a guide; adjust the sauce to your taste.

Serves
4 people
Preparation
About 25 minutes
Marinating
At least 30 minutes
Cooking
About 20 minutes

Ingredients

Pork & marinade

  • 400 g ground pork
  • 300 g pork belly, thinly sliced
  • 2 tbsp fish sauce
  • 1 tbsp brown sugar
  • 2 shallots and 3 garlic cloves, finely minced
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil, plus a little for the grill
  • ½ tsp ground black pepper

Dipping sauce

  • 300 ml warm water
  • 3 tbsp fish sauce
  • 2 tbsp sugar
  • 2 tbsp lime juice, or more to taste
  • 1 garlic clove, minced; sliced chili to taste

Quick pickles

  • 1 small carrot and 150 g peeled green papaya, thinly sliced (or use extra carrot)
  • 3 tbsp rice vinegar
  • 1 tbsp sugar and ¼ tsp salt

To serve

  • 300 g dried rice vermicelli
  • 1 small lettuce, leaves separated
  • 1 handful each of mint and coriander
  • Optional: perilla, Thai basil, and lime wedges

Step by step

  1. Marinate the pork

    Mix the marinade's fish sauce, brown sugar, shallots, garlic, oil, and pepper. Divide between the ground pork and pork belly in separate bowls. Mix well, cover, and refrigerate for at least 30 minutes.

  2. Prepare the pickles

    Stir the rice vinegar, sugar, and salt together until dissolved. Toss with the sliced carrot and green papaya. Leave for about 20 minutes while you prepare the other ingredients, stirring occasionally, then drain.

  3. Mix the dipping sauce

    Dissolve the sauce's sugar in the warm water. Add the fish sauce, lime juice, garlic, and chili. Taste and adjust: more water softens the saltiness, lime adds tang, and sugar adds sweetness. Set aside in a clean serving bowl.

  4. Cook the noodles & wash the greens

    Cook the vermicelli according to its package instructions, rinse with cool water, and drain well. Wash the lettuce and herbs, then arrange them on a serving plate.

  5. Shape & grill

    Shape the ground pork into small, flat patties about 1 cm thick. Lightly oil a grill or grill pan and heat to medium-high. Cook the patties and pork slices in batches, turning until browned and cooked through, roughly 4–5 minutes per side for the patties. Thickness and heat affect timing: use a food thermometer to check that ground-pork patties reach 71°C (160°F), and whole-cut pork reaches 63°C (145°F) followed by a 3-minute rest. Discard any leftover raw-pork marinade. See USDA cooking temperatures.

  6. Bring everything to the table

    Divide the dipping sauce among four bowls. Add the drained pickles and cooked pork. Serve straight away with the noodles and greens on the side, so everyone can build each bite.

The best part

How to enjoy Bún Chả

01

Dip a little at a time

Take a small portion of vermicelli and dip it into the sauce with the pork and pickles.

02

Add something fresh

Include lettuce and a few herbs with each mouthful for a mix of smoky, soft, and crisp textures.

03

Find your balance

Add chili or lime to taste, and enjoy the pork while it is warm from the grill.
