Quang Nam - Da Nang Specialty

Mi Quang – Flavors of Central Vietnam

Today, Mi Quang is renowned across all regions from North to South, but to experience a truly authentic bowl with rich, savory noodles, one must look to Central Vietnam in the historic lands of Quang Nam and Da Nang.

1. Origin of Mi Quang

Mi Quang is a quintessential traditional dish from Quang Nam and Da Nang, affectionately named after its birthplace. The origin of Mi Quang dates back to ancient times when Hoi An was the bustling trade hub of Dang Trong (Inner Realm), ranking among the most prosperous commercial ports in the country.

During that period, Chinese merchants settled in Hoi An, introducing culinary traditions steeped in Chinese style. The people of ancient Quang embraced these noodle-making techniques, thoughtfully adapting them to local preferences and native ingredients. Over generations, this fusion evolved into the iconic Mi Quang we know today, embodying the resilient spirit, generosity, and cultural soul of the people of Quang Nam.

Visiting Quang Nam, you will find Mi Quang eateries on nearly every corner. On every milestone occasion or family gathering, locals will warmly treat you to a steaming bowl of their beloved specialty.

2. Characteristics & Flavor

Although Quang Nam and Da Nang later split into two separate administrative jurisdictions, their cultural traditions and daily lifestyles remain deeply intertwined. Da Nang's Mi Quang carries the same rustic, authentic flavor, but with greater exposure to international tourists, elevating the global prestige of this regional specialty.

Mi Quang noodles are crafted from finely milled rice flour, steam-cooked into thin sheets on a hot griddle, then sliced into flat ribbons approximately 2mm thick. The noodles possess a gentle tenderness with subtle chewiness, either retaining pure white rice color or tinted vibrant gold with natural turmeric. Unlike Pho or Bun, the concentrated broth (nuoc nhuong) is served very shallowly, just enough to moisten the noodles with sweet, savory essence extracted from shrimp, pork, and bones.

3. Historical Development

Through centuries of culinary evolution, Mi Quang has expanded beyond its original pork and shrimp pairing. Modern diners can savor numerous creative variations, including free-range chicken Mi Quang, frog Mi Quang, tender pork ribs, snakehead fish, or delicate vegetarian options.

Regardless of modern twists, the quintessential accompaniments remain unchanged: crisp toasted sesame rice crackers, an abundant platter of fresh herbs featuring finely shredded banana blossom, roasted crushed peanuts, and a crunchy, spicy fresh green chili that provides an unmistakable Central Vietnamese kick.

4. Key Ingredients

Noodles & Protein
  • Fresh flat rice noodles (white or turmeric gold)
  • Fresh river or sea prawns, deveined
  • Savory pork belly, thinly sliced
  • Boiled quail eggs or chicken eggs
  • Pork marrow bones for simmering natural broth
Seasoning & Garnish
  • Shallot bulbs (cu nen), annatto oil, paprika
  • Roasted crushed peanuts, toasted sesame rice crackers
  • Fresh herb basket: banana blossom, mint, lettuce, fish herb
  • Fresh lime wedges, fiery green chilies, pure fish sauce

5. How to Prepare Mi Quang

5.1. Step 1: Ingredient Preparation

Rinse the prawns thoroughly, trim whiskers, and devein along the back. Clean the pork belly and slice into bite-sized pieces. Marinate both meat and prawns with crushed shallot bulbs (cu nen), minced garlic, black pepper, seasonings, a touch of sugar, and premium fish sauce for 20-30 minutes. Wash all fresh herbs thoroughly, soak in light salt water, and drain dry.

5.2. Step 2: Sautéing the Toppings

Heat annatto oil in a pan and sauté crushed shallot bulbs until fragrant and golden. Add the marinated pork belly and stir-fry over high heat until browned. Add the prawns and quail eggs, tossing gently until the ingredients absorb the rich reddish-gold sheen of the annatto oil and release an irresistible aroma.

5.3. Step 3: Simmering the Broth

Pour the clear pork bone stock into the sautéed mixture and simmer gently over low heat for 15-20 minutes, allowing the natural sweetness and savory seasonings to meld into a rich essence. Adjust the seasoning so the broth is slightly more concentrated than ordinary soup, as Mi Quang is meant to be served with only a small ladle of broth.

5.4. Step 4: Plating & Serving

Place a bed of assorted fresh herbs and shredded banana blossom at the bottom of the bowl. Lay a generous serving of fresh noodles over the greens. Top with tender pork, prawns, and quail eggs, then ladle just enough steaming broth to barely cover the bottom third of the noodles. Garnish with roasted crushed peanuts, chopped scallions, crumbled sesame rice crackers, a squeeze of fresh lime, and a bite of crisp green chili for the ultimate Central Vietnamese feast.
